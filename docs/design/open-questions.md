# Questions — resolved

These were open; Rob's answers are now part of the design.

1. **Telling adults.** You can talk to adults, and sometimes you definitely
   should — serious or repeated bullying is worth reporting, and reporting it
   works. It also has costs: the bully's friends may resent you, and someone
   who complains about minor things loses status and gets tagged a *snitch*.
   See [adults](signals-and-gossip.md#adults).
2. **Romance.** Gentle and PG, nothing physical. It should feel like a special
   friendship: crushes, asking someone to the dance, "going out." The
   face-saving invite ladder applies. See
   [romance](signals-and-gossip.md#romance).
3. **Gender, race, sexuality.** Boys and girls, who mostly prefer friends of
   their own gender but not entirely. Race appears only in names and character
   appearance, with no effect on behavior or friendships. Sexuality is not
   modeled; crushes default to the other gender, with no labels.
4. **Physical aggression.** It exists but is mild (a shove). It makes people
   like you less, and it is rarely the right move.
5. **The player's character.** Built like a D&D character: a fixed budget of
   points spread across abilities, so you can't be good at everything. Every
   student uses the same budget. No build should give a clear advantage, and
   the lesson is that everyone is good at something and nobody is good at
   everything. See [people](people.md#abilities-a-fixed-budget).
6. **Simulation size.** Start with 200 students (one grade) and measure how
   fast it runs. If needed later, the player's grade gets full detail and the
   others less.
7. **Teaching principles.** Only in the weekly debrief, and gently — as a short
   explanation of something that just happened, never a lecture.
8. **Moving around.** Click on the map to walk quickly up to people; the
   interaction itself is the card game. See
   [visuals](visuals-and-tech.md#look).
