# The fog, the notebook and the debrief

**Summary.** You see, live, how much each person likes (♥) and respects (★)
you — once they've shown it. What stays hidden is what people will *do* next,
what they hear about you secondhand, and who knows which stories. The notebook
keeps track for you, and the weekly recap shows the bigger picture, including
what happened behind your back.

## What you can see

- **♥ and ★ for each person, live.** In a conversation, each person's card
  shows how much they like you and how much they respect you (−100 to 100),
  with what your last move changed (+3, −2). It works like a card game's
  stats: you see the effect of what you play straight away.
- **But only once they've shown it.** Someone you've just met shows "?" until
  they react where you can see it (reserved people show less, so they stay
  "?" longer), say something to you directly, or you get to know each other
  well. Some people keep their cards close.
- Where people are, who they are with, and what they do within your sight and
  earshot; body language under each line.
- Stories you have been told, and who told them.
- Public events: games, plays, announcements.

**Hidden:** what someone will do next (whether they say yes, laugh, make room
— there's luck in it), what people hear about you secondhand, who knows which
story, and everyone's traits and private opinions.

## The notebook

Your knowledge, drawn for you — built only from what you have observed, so it
can be wrong.

- **People cards.** For each person you've met: name, face, where you usually
  see them, activities, guessed traits ("seems shy? — two clues"), interests
  you've learned, tags you've heard, and your history together.
- **Social map.** A graph of the people you know. Lines are relationships you
  have observed (thicker = seen together more often; dashed = rumor only).
  Clusters show your *guessed* groups.
- **Your circles.** Rings by closeness: close, friends, friendly, known. You can
  pin up to about 15 people as "my people" to keep focus. This is how a big
  school becomes a manageable group.
- **Faces in the crowd.** Everyone else is a face with at most a tag ("the
  loud junior from the soccer team").
- **Rumors log**, with source and how much you trust it.
- **Favors and promises** — who owes whom.

## Tells and trait guesses

Each trait shows through noisy behaviors: short answers, eye contact, laughing,
interrupting, checking a phone, flinching. The notebook turns what you've seen
into guesses with a confidence level. The debrief sometimes confirms one
("Priya is really shy, not cold").

## The weekly debrief

At the end of every school week (school time; a week can pass quickly in real
time):

- **Highlights** — the three to five biggest changes in your relationships and
  standing.
- **What really happened** — for each highlight, the hidden chain of cause and
  effect: *"You laughed when Tyler mocked Sam. Priya saw it. Priya told Jordan
  and Mei. Sam now avoids you."*
- **Partial reveal** of true regard for your circle, as simple icons: likes
  you, trusts you, respects you, wary of you.
- **Your standing** — status within your groups and your grade, its
  [foundation](regard-and-status.md#the-foundation-prestige-versus-dominance)
  (respect versus fear), and your backing (who would defend you), charted
  week to week.
- **Principles unlocked** — a codex entry the first time a principle visibly
  mattered: "Signals are worth what they cost," "Leave an out," "Don't give
  them a stage." This is the only place principles are taught. Each one is a
  sentence or two, explaining something that just happened to you — gentle,
  never a lecture, never a "you should have."
- **Ambitions** — progress against your goals.

## End of semester report

Close friends, backing, status and its foundation, activity achievements,
grades, wellbeing, and ambitions met. There is no single way to win.
